**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Visual/Hook:** A warm, slightly editorial photo of an intimate gathering space — think a private library or a candlelit back room with mismatched chairs. Inviting, not intimidating. Or a simple text-on-dark-background graphic: "No, it's not a cult. (We can't even agree on a wine.)"

**Post Copy:**

**No, it's not a cult.**

I get this question every time I tell someone about The Secret Society of Humans Being. So let me get it out of the way.

There's no leader. No manifesto. No chanting. Nobody's going to ask you to sell essential oils or find your "divine purpose."

It's a room. With candles. And people.

Here's what it actually is:

A monthly, phone-free gathering in intimate speakeasy-style venues. Members show up. We read. We make things. We talk. Sometimes we sit in silence. Sometimes someone reads a poem badly and we love it.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

I started this because I noticed something in my own life and in the lives of the smartest, most "connected" people I know: we are drowning in communication and starving for connection.

We have Slack channels and Discord servers and group chats and followers. And somehow we're lonelier than any generation before us.

The Secret Society isn't anti-technology. I'm posting this on LinkedIn, clearly. But there's a difference between using tools and being used by them. And I think a lot of us crossed that line a while ago and are just now noticing.

So once a month, we leave the tools at the door. We walk into a room where nobody is recording anything. Where nothing is "content." Where you can be terrible at watercolor and that's the whole point.
