# Pre-Launch Social Posts — Self-Identification & Trust Building

These posts do NOT mention the offer. They exist to help the ideal buyer see themselves, feel seen, and start paying attention.

---

## Post 1: Self-Identification

**Platform:** Instagram (carousel or text post)

**Copy:**

You might be a Human Being in a world of Human Doings if...

You're the one who puts your phone face-down at dinner. Not because someone told you to. Because you actually want to hear what your friend is saying.

You still write things by hand. Not for aesthetics. Because something about pen on paper makes your brain work differently.

You miss doing things badly. Painting something ugly. Playing guitar with clumsy fingers. Making a meal without photographing it.

You've left a party early because the conversations were all surface and you'd rather be alone than pretend that's enough.

You've read the same paragraph four times because your attention span got stolen somewhere between 2016 and now, and you're quietly furious about it.

You don't want to "unplug" as a trend. You want to plug back into the thing you actually are.

If you read this whole thing without checking another tab, hi. I see you.

**Suggested visual:** Simple text on a warm off-white or cream background. No graphics. Handwritten font or typewriter style. If carousel, one point per slide with the last slide being "hi. I see you." on its own.

**Hashtags:** #analoglife #presentliving #digitaldetox #slowliving #offlineistheNewluxury #humanbeingnothumandoing

## Post 2: Myth-Busting / Contrarian Take

**Platform:** X/Twitter (thread, 4-5 posts)

**Copy:**

Unpopular opinion: You don't have a productivity problem. You have a presence problem.

We optimized everything. Our mornings. Our meals. Our sleep. Our friendships. Our rest. We turned recovery into a KPI.

And we've never been more exhausted.

You know what nobody optimized? Staring out a window for twenty minutes. Sitting with a friend in comfortable silence. Making something with your hands that serves no purpose except that it felt good to make.

"Connected" is the biggest lie of the decade. You have 1,100 followers and no one to call when something actually goes wrong. You have 47 unread group chats and you're lonely. You know what your ex ate for lunch but you don't know what your own body is feeling right now.

The most radical thing you can do in 2026 isn't another system or protocol or habit stack. It's sitting still in a room without reaching for a screen and finding out you're still in there.

**Suggested visual/hook:** Plain text tweets. No images. Let the words carry it. Pin the first tweet if it gets traction.

## Post 3: Quick Tip / Framework

**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Copy:**

**Try this once this week. Just once.**

Pick one evening. Any evening. Doesn't have to be tonight.

At 7pm, put your phone in a drawer. Not on the counter face-down. Not on airplane mode in your pocket. In a drawer. In another room.

Then do whatever you want.

Cook something. Read a physical book. Sit on your porch. Call someone — on a landline if you still have one, or just before you put the phone away. Draw something terrible. Take a bath. Stare at a wall. It genuinely does not matter.

What matters is this: at some point during the evening, you're going to feel a pull. A phantom buzz. An itch to check something. You'll feel it in your chest or your hands.

Don't judge it. Just notice it. That's the interesting part.

Because that itch isn't curiosity. It's not FOMO. It's a habit that replaced something else. And the thing it replaced is still in there, waiting.

Most people who try this report the first 30 minutes are uncomfortable. By 90 minutes, something shifts. They describe it as "quiet" — but not silence. More like hearing your own frequency again after years of static.

One evening. One drawer. See what happens.

**Suggested visual:** Clean, minimal text graphic with the core tip: "Phone in a drawer. 7pm. One evening. See what happens." White text on a muted navy or forest green background. Professional but warm.

**Hashtags:** #leadership #presence #intentionalliving #focus #wellbeing #digitalwellness

## Post 4: Vulnerable / Honest Story

**Platform:** Facebook

**Copy:**

I need to tell you about the moment I scared myself.

I was sitting on my couch on a Saturday morning. Beautiful day outside. Coffee getting cold next to me. And I was watching a video of someone making pottery. Not because I wanted to learn pottery. Just... watching. On autoplay. For forty-five minutes.

And then I looked up and caught my reflection in the TV screen — this person hunched over a phone, mouth slightly open, eyes glazed — and I didn't recognize myself.

Not in a dramatic, movie-moment way. In a slow, creeping way. Like realizing you've been holding your breath and you don't know when you stopped breathing normally.

I used to make things. I used to read books in one sitting. I used to sit in coffee shops and write in notebooks and eavesdrop on conversations and feel like the world was interesting and I was part of it.

Somewhere along the way I became a person who watches other people do those things. On a 6-inch screen. While my own hands do nothing.

The thing that got me wasn't guilt. It was grief. I was grieving a version of myself that I'd let dissolve so gradually I didn't notice until she was almost gone.

I don't have a neat ending for this. I didn't throw my phone in a river or move to a cabin. I just started doing small, dumb things. I bought a sketchbook and drew badly. I left my phone at home when I walked to the grocery store. I sat on my front steps and did absolutely nothing for fifteen minutes and it was the hardest thing I'd done in months.

I'm not "healed." I'm not preaching. I'm just saying — if you read that first part and felt something tighten in your chest, you're not alone. And you're not broken. You're just a human who got swept up in something that was designed to sweep you up.

The fact that you noticed? That's the whole first step.

**Suggested visual:** No graphic. Just the raw text. If anything, a simple personal photo — a coffee cup, a notebook, a window. Nothing staged. The vulnerability IS the hook.

## Post 5: Belief Shift

**Platform:** TikTok (text on screen / talking to camera)

**Copy (on screen or spoken):**

The difference between people who feel alive and people who feel numb is not talent. Or money. Or time.

It's this:

People who feel alive do things that have no ROI.

They draw pictures nobody sees. They walk without a podcast. They cook without a recipe. They sit with friends and don't check the time.

They let themselves be bad at things. Slow at things. Unproductive.

They waste time on purpose. And it turns out that's not wasting time at all.

That's the thing we used to call living.

**Suggested visual/hook:** Direct to camera, natural lighting, no ring light. Or: black screen with white text appearing line by line, slight delay between each. Lo-fi music underneath — something warm and acoustic, not trendy. The hook on screen in the first frame: "Why some people feel alive and others feel numb." Keep it under 45 seconds.

**Hashtags:** #presenceoverperfection #analoglife #slowliving #dopaminedetox #phonefree #humansbeinghumans #feelalive

## Posting Strategy Notes

- Space these out over 7-10 days
- Post the self-identification (Post 1) and belief shift (Post 5) first — these get the highest engagement and self-selection
- The vulnerable story (Post 4) works best mid-sequence when some trust is already built
- Watch comments carefully — the people who say "I feel attacked" or "why is this so accurate" are your future buyers
- Engage genuinely in comments. No "DM me for more info." Just be a person talking to people. That IS the pre-launch.
